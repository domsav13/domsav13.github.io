For my capstone project, my responsibilities included creating a software and data architecture for our spotlight controls, working with IMUs as a feedback sensor, implementing interprocess communications in the electronics, designing user interfaces, and supporting assembly and manufacturing.

### Scope and Requirements

For this project (funded by University Research Foundation), a maritime spotlight that will track and target buoys or other in-water targets to assist in "man overboard" searches and illuminate hazards will be designed, built, and tested. While some spotlights are effective with manual operations, the goal of this prototype is to implement an automatic system in which the light orients itself for stability and to sweep a vessel's navigational history to point to objects of interest. Building on a previous prototype, research was conducted in areas such as water resistance, power management, motor selection, and control systems to create a more compact, reliable, and user-friendly product. 

Due to the sponsor's expertise and guidance, the following functional requirements were provided by them during preliminary conversations:

- The spotlight must provide location data without resetting to a specific position
- The sensors must provide information for self-stabilization every 0.1 seconds
- The IMU must provide Euler orientation data that is accurate to 5 degrees
- All electronics must utilize a shared memory with respective read/write permissions to data
- All power must be provided by a 12-24 V source
- The power source must supply 91.2 W of power (calculated) for all electronics
- The light will be able to pan +360 and -360 degrees and tilt +90 and -45 degrees
- The motors must be able to accelerate from 60 degrees per second in one direction to 60 degrees per second in the other direction within 0.2 seconds
- The mechanical design must prevent backdriving in the motion of the light
- The motors will point the light to $\pm$ 0.2 degrees of accuracy via absolute encoders
- The pan and tilt motor commands must be able to occur simultaneously
- All electronic components must reside within the base and be structurally secured
- The spotlight must be dimensioned for mounting to the deck of a boat (18-65 foot vessels)
- The external housing will be able to withstand extreme maritime operations

For the overall scope of the project, it is important to understand the inputs from a multifunctional display (MFD) or chart plotter, which provide navigational data, vessel history, absolute GPS position (longitude, latitude, altitude), velocity, and attitude (pitch, roll, yaw). These sources also supply absolute target coordinates, making coordinate system management critical. Both the vessel and target operate in absolute geographic frames whereas the spotlight operates in a local frame, assuming shared latitude and longitude with the vessel.

### Final Design and Architectures

The final design shows two independent drive systems (for pan and tilt motion), electronics boxes, 3D printed housings, and key mechanical components. For tilting, a worm gear system is used to transfer torque from the tilt motor to the drive shaft. A direct drive is used with the pan motor to ensure proper rotation of the light.

2 cad images

The final design was guided by a system architecture encompassing the scope of mechanical and control system design. The components are divided into software and hardware, and the scope is represented by four primary subsystems: the means of pointing, interfaces and wire management, power and motor control, and sensors and data.

system architecture image

The circuit for the spotlight electronics shows the wiring and power sources for the components: the Arduino MEGA, 2 absolute rotary encoders, converters for distributing correct power, motor drivers, and stepper motors. Not pictured is the Raspberry Pi 4 which is connected serially to the Arduino MEGA and runs on an additional power source.
